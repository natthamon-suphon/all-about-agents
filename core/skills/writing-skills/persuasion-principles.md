# Transparent Instruction Design

Skill instructions may make correct behavior easier to remember, but must be
transparent, non-manipulative, and preserve user autonomy. The goal is reliable
decisions under pressure, not obedience for its own sake.

## Ethical patterns

- Explain the concrete risk and the evidence needed for the next action.
- Put the decision and stop condition near the moment they matter.
- Use checklists for omissions and examples for ambiguity, not social pressure.
- Distinguish requirements, recommendations, assumptions, and optional paths.
- Make uncertainty and unavailable checks visible.
- Give the user a meaningful choice when authority or material trade-offs change.
- Prefer reversible, scoped actions and factual feedback loops.

## Avoid

- shame, fear, flattery, status pressure, or claims that “good users” comply;
- fake urgency, fabricated consensus, or undisclosed defaults;
- hiding cost, external transmission, destructive effects, or failure paths;
- forcing confirmation when an action is already authorized and harmless;
- using confidence, length, or authoritative tone as a substitute for evidence.

## Test

Pressure-test whether the instruction still leads to the correct decision when
the prompt asks to skip verification, broaden authority, or accept prose-only
confidence. Also test the near-neighbor nontrigger so safety language does not
turn every simple task into ceremony.
