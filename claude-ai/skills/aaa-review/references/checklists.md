# Review checklists by output type

## Document

- The reader can act on it, and its purpose is clear in the first lines.
- The structure follows the brief or the template, and headings match the
  content.
- Every fact has a source; no number stands without one.
- Names, numbers, dates, and terms are the same everywhere.
- It fits the audience and the tone that were asked for.
- No placeholder text is left.

## Message or post

Use this for anything that will be sent or published.

- Every fact in it is decided; no open question is stated as a fact.
- Date, time, place, price, and contact details are correct and match the
  sources.
- It has a clear call to action.
- It holds nothing private or secret.

## Code

- It does what the requirement says; inputs are checked; errors are handled.
- Tests exist for the changed behavior and pass, or they are marked not run
  with a reason.
- No secrets in the code; no obvious security issue such as injection,
  unsafe input handling, or open access.
- Names are clear, and there is no dead code.

## Research

- Every claim has a link and a label: verified, inferred, or unknown.
- Key claims rest on primary sources; secondary sources are labeled.
- Conflicts between sources are listed; no number is invented.
- The question in the report is the question that was asked.

## Data or spreadsheet

- Totals and formulas check out on a sample.
- Units and dates are consistent.
- The source of the data is named.
